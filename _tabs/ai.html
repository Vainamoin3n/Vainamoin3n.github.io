02

Job search & applications

Personal workflow

From polishing words to understanding the opportunity.

3 hours → reduced to 30 minutes
My estimated preparation time per tailored application, including review and editing. Submission time excluded.

The biggest change is where I put my attention. I used to spend much of the application process choosing the right words. With the agent helping me structure and draft, I can focus more on learning about the company, understanding the position, and deciding whether it is a good fit.

The workflow searches for roles based on my skills, researches the company and position, and asks me targeted questions before drafting. Those questions help me clarify why I want the role and which experiences are relevant. I then review and edit the application in my own voice.

The shift in focus: The time saving matters, but the shift in focus matters more: understanding the opportunity and why I want it, then finding the words to explain that.

The tension I still feel about authenticity

I still have mixed feelings about this. Even when the experience and motivation are mine, I worry that using AI makes my application feel less genuine to the recruiter. With so many AI-assisted applications coming in, I can understand why they might question what they read. At the same time, I feel at a disadvantage if I don't use these tools. I try to navigate that tension by keeping the content grounded in my experience and editing it in my own voice.

World of Warships

Personal project

Turning match history into useful coaching.

50+
players coached
300+
matches analysed

A set of agents converts replays, analyses matches, and prepares coaching and strategy advice for a French-speaking clan. Reports support both in-season review and end-of-season reflection.

During the season

Review individual matches, player roles, opponent behaviour, and coordination. Turn the analysis into advice players can understand and use, with updates shared through Discord.

At the end of the season

Bring matches together to examine patterns over time. Look across several measures of a player's contribution to identify questions and priorities for coaching.

Explore a single-match review: targeting and coordination
What I learned about evidence and communication

An agent once claimed to analyse a video it could not actually read. Comparing its claims with what I saw exposed the problem. I also learned that advice only helps if it is specific and people understand it: clear French worked better than invented gaming jargon.

The workflow uses written resources, spreadsheets, and video material to build its understanding. I still need to check the evidence behind a confident answer and make the result understandable to the people using it.

Unity · Coding agents · MCP

On pause

A playable prototype is only the beginning.

82 tasks
worked through across the project
22 milestones
across the wider project

The week refers to the first playable prototype. The task and milestone counts cover the broader project.

I wanted to build a tower defense game inspired by Line Tower Wars from the Warcraft III modding community. I started with a roadmap, agent-written scripts, and manual changes in the Unity editor. I later connected the agent directly to Unity through Model Context Protocol (MCP), so it could work with scenes and objects.

The product lesson: completing tasks and getting the core loop working did not make the game enjoyable. The prototype is functional, but it still needs the details that make someone want to keep playing. I chose to pause it for now.

What I learned about directing agents and enjoying the work

Getting to “somewhat working” is surprisingly fast. Getting all the details right in a game takes much longer. The agent needs clear direction and someone who checks what actually happens in the game. It also helped me improve how I communicate with agents.

At one point, I felt like a pawn in my own project. Much of my role had become checking the result, then saying, “OK, next task on the roadmap.” Progress was fast, but I was enjoying the work less. That taught me to pay attention to which parts I want to do myself, so that building with agents stays enjoyable.

I may come back to this game or start a different one. Either way, it gave me experience of the gap between a working prototype and a game worth publishing.
